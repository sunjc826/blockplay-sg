# Open-world vehicles

Expedition facility rules now govern normal infantry waves and fleet replacements: operational command posts, garages and helipads are required, with shared supply costs and local deployment safety. Off-map combat support remains available. See [facilities](FACILITIES.md) for these overrides to the original reinforcement and replacement behavior described below.

Each loaded district has one Utility 01 and one Falcon 01. The fleet uses the district's authored training spawns when clear; blocked pads relocate nearby, checking hulls, street obstacles, traversal structures, rotor clearance and district bounds, and keeping the pad away from locals and the arrival point. Loot placement avoids the parked hulls. Both appear on the district map. Vehicles belong to the current district session; crossing a checkpoint remains on foot and loading another district creates its fleet anew.

E boards/exits (a looked-at loot item takes priority on foot); V changes to a vacant seat without unloading the crew. L switches between first-person and third-person views in every seat; Q / RMB aims in first person. Touch has a view switch plus contextual Board/Exit, seat, brake, climb and descend controls. The normal mounted hull, ammunition, crew list and aiming display also work in expeditions. Passengers can fire, aim, change and reload their equipped firearms using their own ammunition, while the gunner controls the mounted weapon. Passenger rounds leave their own hull safely and remain subject to host hit and ammunition checks. Infantry supplies do not refill a hull.

Collision damage uses approach speed into the blocked movement axes after acceleration or braking, before the collision stops that motion. Bumps up to 5 m/s are harmless; harder impacts deal `2 × (approach speed − 5)²` hull damage (50 HP at 10 m/s, 200 HP at 15 m/s, 450 HP at 20 m/s). Glancing scrapes use the inward component; helicopter roof/ground contacts use vertical speed. Contact sheds the blocked motion, and continuing to push against a wall at low speed causes no further crash damage. Practice and authoritative arena/expedition vehicles share these rules.

A destroyed, empty hull becomes eligible for replacement after 45 seconds. An empty working vehicle away from its pad becomes eligible after 120 seconds. Replacements restore health and mounted ammunition at the motor pool. A living player within 25 m of either a wreck or the destination pad delays replacement; reclaiming an abandoned working vehicle also requires the player to be at least 60 m away. Living AI within 8 m of either location and occupied/blocked pads delay replacement. A vehicle in use never gets replaced. Training and match resets retain their previous behavior.

Calm, healthy patrol AI without an active movement order within 24 m can approach and board cars, or ride/gun in a helicopter with an existing pilot. Only allied crews share vehicles. AI leaves an unattended vehicle for a player already within 12 m, rallies up to three nearby allies from within 45 m, and waits up to 18 seconds for them to fill the gunner and passenger seats before driving. It waits at least three seconds and requires a gunner; if nobody can man the gun, the driver dismounts and resumes its foot patrol. Once assembled it uses collision-checked local patrol segments at roughly 7 m/s. If there is no usable route or progress for five seconds, it stops and dismounts. This is local patrol steering, not a district road-routing system.

Player pickups use a sustained stop: stay in the driver/pilot seat below 1 m/s for one second, with the helicopter landed and settled. Nearby healthy allies within 24 m approach using infantry collision and board at normal entry range with a clear line to the cab. Unassigned allies and soldiers gathering on that driver may join; hold, move and scatter orders take precedence, and followers of another player keep following their caller. The first available seat is filled, so the gunner comes before passengers; occupied seats are never displaced. A full vehicle, acceleration, driver departure or a seat change cancels further pickup attempts. Reaching a fresh stop starts the one-second invitation again. Pickups can happen under enemy fire and with an empty mounted gun; healing, serious infantry wounds, hull health at or below 35%, and marked strike danger still prevent boarding.

AI riding with a player stays aboard during stops and nearby combat, using the mounted gun or passenger weapons where possible. Switching seats keeps the existing crew aboard and skips occupied seats; if no other seat is empty, nothing changes. AI never fills an empty driver seat while a player remains aboard. If the player switches out of the driver seat and then the last player leaves the driverless vehicle, the remaining crew also unloads safely.

An AI driver stops and unloads when an enemy is visible within 35 m, when the hull is critically damaged, when a strike threatens it, or when it gets stuck. That includes a lone driver choosing to fight on foot. AI gunners use the mounted gun and its finite ammunition. Mounted AI does not also walk or fire its infantry weapon.

When the driver exits, the whole crew exits together. Every exit is planned against scenery, other hulls, nearby people and the other exits before any seat is freed. Exit candidates clear the vehicle at any heading and use infantry floor/headroom checks, so a walkable pavement is a valid dismount surface. The route out is checked too, preventing exits through walls. A blocked or moving crew stays aboard until it can unload safely. AI waits 12 seconds before considering boarding again. A lost/disconnected driver also makes the remaining crew stop and unload; an airborne helicopter without a pilot first descends, keeping passengers aboard until a ground-level exit is possible.

Shared hulls, riders and cameras use the same interpolated presentation between authority ticks, with shortest-path heading interpolation and immediate resets for replacements. Riders never obstruct the chase camera.

Helicopters remain player-piloted; autonomous AI flight and navigation to a landing zone are outside this change. AI supports helicopter passenger and gunner seats.

Validation: `arena-vehicle-pickup.test.ts` covers player pickup timing, follow/order precedence, moving/full/driverless cancellation, combat, crew dismounts and reboard cooldown, seat switching, blocked approaches and helicopter flight. `world-vehicles.test.ts` covers every district's motor pool plus crew exits, blocked exits, AI patrol/combat, faction boarding, abandonment, wreck replacement and training isolation. `scripts/world-vehicle-smoke.mjs` exercises expedition boarding, driving, mounted and passenger firing, first-person views, switching seats, diagonal-parking dismounts, and mobile controls with the local development server and an isolated Chrome debugging endpoint.

Verified locally: vehicle/control regression tests, district scene checks, TypeScript checking, production build, and the desktop/mobile browser smoke all passed. The browser smoke reported no runtime errors or remote map requests.
