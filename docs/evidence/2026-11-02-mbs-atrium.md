# MBS hotel atrium reference review — 2 November 2026

The earlier ground interiors used generic low ceilings and furnishings. This pass
rebuilds their spatial structure from two inspected published photographs:

- [Timothy Hursley’s hotel lobby photograph, President’s Design Award](https://pda.designsingapore.org/award-recipients/2011/marina-bay-sands-integrated-resort-singapore/): tall void, ivory gallery edges, inclined glazed structure, crossing bridge and suspended wirework.
- [CL3’s original Marina Bay Sands interior portfolio](https://www.cl3.com/marina-bay-sands): curved lounge islands, generous circulation, tall tree pots and warm recesses beneath the galleries.

Source URLs, SHA-256 digests and narrow visual acceptance scopes are retained in
[the review manifest](../../reconstruction/marina-atrium-review.json). Cached
photographs remain local research references; they are not shipped image assets.
No Google captures or paid API requests were made.

## Implemented interpretation

One 80m-long, approximately 24m-high compressed atrium joins the three towers.
The former 4.5m ceiling boxes are removed, and upper hotel shell/collision starts
above the atrium. Repeated gallery fronts, hotel wing envelopes, splayed piers,
inclined glass walls, skylight ribs and two overhead bridges establish its shape.
The central lounge uses curved low partitions, tables, upholstered chairs and a
bar island; tapered pots contain branching trees with instanced leaf geometry.
A lightweight wire sculpture studies the reference’s airy visual character.
Ground reception alcoves and stone lift returns connect the existing express
cars to the architecture. A broad route beside the lounge and multiple entrances
keep the ground level traversable. The lift and roof movement repairs are retained.

## Scope and limits

These are photographs of the original design, not a survey of 2026 refurbishment.
The 2023 official MBS renovation material was consulted in the preceding research,
but its lift-lobby photographs are not accepted implementation evidence here.
The atrium is compressed and authored to fit the existing game district. Gallery
counts, dimensions, lounge layout, sculpture and express-lift placement are
interpretations. Glass express cars remain the agreed gameplay adaptation.
Upper galleries/bridges are scenic and have no new stairs or objectives; their
visible slabs still support a player who lands on them. Ground/roof/lift access
is playable. Furniture and artwork are original procedural game geometry.

Repeated geometry is instanced, glazing and wall panels merged by material,
and the wire study uses one line draw. Lighting accents are emissive materials;
no additional point lights or shadow maps are allocated.
